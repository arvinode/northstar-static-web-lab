Performance is part of the experience.

A long-form sample article for testing reading width, heading rhythm, images and anchor navigation.

Every request has a cost

A browser builds a page from many pieces: the document, stylesheets, scripts, fonts, images, video frames and third-party resources. Each request may involve DNS resolution, connection setup, TLS negotiation, transferring bytes and parsing content.

Some work happens in parallel; other work blocks rendering or competes for bandwidth. The point of a lab is to change one variable at a time and measure its effect rather than guessing from a single score.

Measure the whole journey

Inspect browser developer tools, server access and error logs, response headers, transfer sizes, cache status, compression, time to first byte and page rendering. Repeat tests with warm and cold caches and compare HTTP/1.1 with HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 where your setup supports them.

Fast is not a trick. It is the result of clear priorities and measured trade-offs.

Keep a record

Write down the exact change, the test conditions, the result and the rollback. A useful experiment should be repeatable by another person and should not rely on memory.
